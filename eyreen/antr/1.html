Chapter 1

"Eyreen is a high elf, highborn. With golden hair, a pair of purple eyes and... very beautiful." Jordis said to an old lumber beside the bridge.

He put a hand on his chin and gave her a thoughtful look. For once his eyes actually proved that he is thinking.

"Not that I can remember m'lady. Who is she for you? Lover? Master?"

"My Thane." She lied.

"Well, we don't see no Thane here."

Absurdly, Jordis was not surprised. Her Thane has a penchant for locks and noses for shadows. Most like she was master thief too.

As the man came back to his work, Jordis turned to find her Thane once more.

She did not remember the walk back to the inn. Nor when she started nursing her drink of milk, honey and three yellow eggs mixed in. As she sat alone, she wondered if this whole thing was a folly. The guards swore to her that they had never seen her Thane left through the gate. Nor the dockworkers seen any altmer aboard a ship. The stable boys proved much less helpful.

It's as if her Thane magicked her way and gone. She probably did, Jordis gravely realized.

"Jordis, is that you?" She looked up, Lissete was walking towards her little corner of Winking Skeever. Gods, Jordis didn't even realized her song has finished.

"Gods, where were you yesterday?" Lissete asked, taking a seat beside her.

"Where else?" Jordis said, swallowing her drink. "Looking for my Thane, obviously." She dare not look at Lissete's eyes, it was always filled with pity nowadays.

"Oh," Lissete pursed her lips, then forcing it into a smile. "Where did you look this time?" Lissete said, in that stupid false cheer of hers.

"Dragon Bridge." Jordis might have said that with too much force, but she need all that force with her right now. "You remember that Moth Priest who came to court a few weeks past? He was attacked apparently, his guards and servants dead just outside the bridge itself! And then some guards from there said that two women were poking around looking for him too! One of them is said to ride a daedric warhorse!"

It was clear even for Jordis what Lissete was thinking, they never believe. But not Jordis. "It was her! No other mage can do half what she done."

"I'm sure that was her, but why doesn't she make way to Solitude then?" Lissete's voice was small, yet it subduded Jordis either way.

"She...she has her own reasons, she was with someone else too y'know! Maybe whatever she did was too important to a warrant a visit." Jordis said, lamely.

Lissete almost said something when Sorex called her away. "I suppose this is it, stay safe Jordis." Lissete gave her a hug, then walk away.

Lissete was singing Dragonborn Comes when Octieve make way to her table, his face his usual reddish drunk.

Before he can open his mouth, Jordis cut him off. "No, I didn't bring any septims for you to borrow."

"That's too bad I guess, but that's not what I meant to do." He said. That gave her a pause. Frankly, Octieve is an old done man. Whatever he 'meant' to do usually involve gambling or drinking, or gambling while drinking. "When you were away, there was an orc here. Big and old, he was wearing this armor I've never seen before. Harassing patrons one by one to join his band of warriors. They call themselves the..." He paused.

"Dawnguard, they were the Dawnguard." Jordis finished.

"Yes! Lady Eyreen might be among them, they are based on Riften too I might add." Octieve said, somehow pleased with himself.

"No, she wouldn't be there." Her Thane has joined a band of warriors before, in her 'wild days' as she put it. It eventually ended with her dueling one of her battle-brother, culminating in him losing an arm and her an exile.

Her Thane never pick up steel again nor she ever told Jordis why she ever fought that duel in the first place. Only stern warning to never join the Companions, nor take up arms against them.

"That's too bad then," he gave her shoulder a small clap. "Hope you find her soon!" Then he too, walk away.

Even if Jordis did find her, what stopping her from doing the same again?

She could not stop her, would not. No, Jordis would beg to bring her along, that's for certain. Outside skyrim if it must. To Cyrodil, across Morrowind, up there into High Rock. Maybe even Alinor, her Thane would find a way. She always did.

"M'lady." She looked up, slower than usual. The man who called her is a handsome breton, a mage from the look of it with his dark robe. Short, with soft hand and hair. Though, there's something unusual about the way he smile that Jordis cannot place.

"May I have this seat?" The man took the chair across her before she could speak. Jordis decided to ignore him.

It did not take long before the man grew bored of her silence.

"M'lady, what are you drinking?" He slurred.

Jordis turned to truly looked at him then. He is smiling at her, an open mouthed smile that revealed his one golden tooth. When Jordis stayed silent he decided it was the right time to introduce himself.

"My name's Sam, Sam Guevenne. Pleasure to meet you m'lady." He said, offering his hand, forcing Jordis to take it.

"Jordis, of Solitude." His hands were rough, with calloused skin, soft flesh and disappointing grip. "What brings you to Solitude, goodman Sam?"

"That's," He smirked. "a pointed question m'lady. I am a mere hedge mage, looking for someone..." His eyes were on her now. "To share a drink with."

He pulled a bottle out of nowhere. A colovian brandy, her Thane's favorite when she was deep in her books. Sometimes she would just sloshing it around in the cup, taking it on an adventure through the house while muttering to herself.

Once, Jordis asked what she was doing. "Why, conferring with my inner council that is." Her Thane proudly said, then sniffed her cup.

She likes their smell more, Jordis realized. That's why even a small bottle could last a moon and more.

Sam already poured a full cup of it before she could object. Then another cup, this one so full it almost spilled over to the table. He gave her the second one.

"Here, so we may wash away your sorrow." His smile was strangely warm now, with a hint of sadness in his eyes. The cup is sticky sweet in her hand, the wine bright jet. A drop of it fell onto the table. When she went to wipe it, she only felt the dry polished wood.

"Thank you goodman Sam..." But she couldn't drink right now, she wanted to say. Yet she was so so tired now, a cup wouldn't hurt...
